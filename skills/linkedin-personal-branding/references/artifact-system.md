# Weekly Artifact System

The content plan is not 60 posts. It is **8 shippable artifacts with posts
orbiting them**. The artifact is the value; the posts are distribution.

## The goal: market-operator positioning

Everything here serves one outcome: **the market comes to treat this person
as an operator-expert** — someone who runs the motions they talk about, has
the scars and the numbers, and ships useful things. Not a commentator, not a
content marketer, not a vendor with opinions.

That dictates the frame for every deliverable:

- **Artifacts are the person's real operating assets, published.** The
  standard their team actually runs, the rubric they actually score with,
  the math they actually present — written in their system's own language
  (their named method, their thresholds, their war stories). "I wrote down
  the standard we run; here it is" — never "I made you a lead magnet."
- **Posts demonstrate judgment, not information.** Information is searchable;
  judgment is what experts have: thresholds ("below 40%, stop and fix the
  ask"), sequences ("fix H before R — it's cheaper"), tradeoffs, failure
  modes, when-this-then-that. Every post should contain a decision the
  reader couldn't have made before reading it.
- **Evidence is lived.** "I've run this across four companies" / "the pattern
  across 31 pipelines I've seen" — first-person operator pattern-recognition,
  drawn from the real evidence file. Cited research and borrowed statistics
  read as commentary; lived distribution reads as expertise.
- **The expert never sells in the post body.** The week's usefulness IS the
  promotion. CTAs stay small and on-platform; the offer appears as the
  natural next rung for whoever wants the operator's help running what was
  just published — and is named plainly only where the ladder says so
  (mid-campaign and the offer week).

## The rule

One artifact per week, **generated by this skill as a real file** in
`artifacts/` — not a concept with an hours estimate. A tool, template,
checklist, audit, worksheet, teardown kit, or guide the ICP can use the day
they get it. Then the week's posts work it from different angles.

If an artifact can't be genuinely useful standing alone, it's the wrong
artifact. Pick again.

## Anchor everything on the offer

Before designing artifacts, the offer must be explicit (Step 0 input):

- **offer.name** — what the person ultimately sells (e.g., "20-minute
  pipeline review → Audienti")
- **offer.primary_url** — where it converts
- **offer.cta_keyword** — the DM/comment keyword used across the plan
- **value ladder** — the artifact sequence is a ladder: early weeks build the
  worldview (free standards, diagnostics), middle weeks prove and equip
  (math, audits, templates), final weeks assemble the system and land one
  step short of the offer — so taking the offer is the obvious next rung,
  not a leap.

Every artifact ends with an `offer_bridge`: one honest sentence connecting it
to the next rung (often the next week's artifact; in weeks 4 and 8, the offer
itself). No bait-and-switch: the artifact must deliver fully without the
offer.

## Artifact types

`Guide` · `Template` · `Checklist` · `Calculator` (worksheet with formulas) ·
`Audit` (scored checks with thresholds) · `Framework` · `Teardown` (rubric +
before/after examples) · `Worksheet` · `Playbook` · `Swipe File`

Vary the type across weeks. Two audits back-to-back is a lecture; an audit
then a template is a system.

## What makes an artifact real (quality bar)

- **Usable in under 30 minutes** the day it arrives, by the ICP persona, with
  data they already have.
- **Opinionated**: thresholds, not considerations. "Below 40% hold rate, stop
  prospecting and fix the ask" — not "hold rates vary."
- **Complete**: every check has a pass/fail line; every template field has an
  example entry; every formula has a worked scenario.
- **Grounded**: numbers and mechanisms come from the evidence (the person's
  real method, site, history) — never invented case data. Composite examples
  must be labeled as composites.
- **Self-contained**: delivers value with zero further purchases. The bridge
  to the offer is one line at the end, not a hole in the middle.

## Weekly promotion arc

Each week's posts orbit that week's artifact with distinct roles:

| Day | Role | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `tease` | The problem the artifact kills — pain named with specifics, artifact promised |
| 2 | `drop` | The artifact ships. What's inside (concretely), who it's for, keyword CTA |
| 3 | `application` | Walk ONE piece deeply — a check, a formula, a template field — with a worked example |
| 4 | `objection` | The smartest reason someone won't use it — taken seriously, then dismantled |
| 5 | `proof` | The mechanism or number behind it — why this works, evidence-grounded |
| 6 | `operator` | The founder/operator lens — the story or scar that produced the artifact |
| 7 | `activation` | Harvest: what did people find? Reply to results, invite the next step |

Different posts, same spine. A reader who sees any two posts that week gets
value AND knows the artifact exists. Days 57–60 are the offer week: recap the
ladder, restate the standing offer, no new artifact (`role: recap`).

## All killer, no filler — the post bar

Every post must clear ALL of these before it's `post_ready`:

1. **The extract test**: the post contains at least one specific, usable
   thing — a threshold, a check, a formula, a step sequence, a before/after,
   a named failure mode. If the reader can't DO something differently
   tomorrow, it's filler.
2. **The forward test**: would the ICP persona forward this to their team
   with the note "we should do this"? Inspiration doesn't get forwarded;
   instructions do.
3. **The strip test**: cover the author's name. Could any vendor in the
   category have posted it? If yes, kill it. Expertise is specificity.
4. **The conversation test**: the post takes a position someone informed
   could disagree with, or surfaces a number someone would question. Posts
   that everyone nods at move nothing.
5. **The operator test**: the post shows judgment exercised — a decision
   rule, a sequencing call, a tradeoff taken — backed by lived evidence from
   the person's actual history. If it could have been written by a smart
   person who'd only read about the problem, it fails. Experts are people
   who've paid for their opinions.

Banned: motivational generalities, restated truisms ("trust matters in
sales"), advice without thresholds, hooks that promise more than the body
delivers, any sentence that survives the strip test unattributed.

The artifact makes this bar reachable: posts extract from it. When a post
feels thin, the fix is to pull a sharper piece of the artifact into it — not
to write better prose around nothing.
